# Wooden SodaCan Lamp
A working table lamp with a hand-built wooden base and a repurposed soda can as the socket riser — built entirely from raw lumber, a threaded rod, and basic shop tools.

## How it was built

The base started as raw wood, run through the planer multiple times to hit the target thickness, then trimmed on the table saw into long planks. A miter saw cut those planks into three wooden squares, which were trimmed to final shape on the table saw and stacked to form the tiered base. Each piece went through a belt sander and jointer to smooth the faces and edges, then a wood router with a round-over bit softened all the edges.

A drill press bored the holes for the wiring and the metal support tube through the center of the base, and a CNC machine routed a hidden channel from that center hole out to the edge so the power cord could exit without being visible. After hand-sanding and staining, a threaded rod was cut to length and run through the wood and a cut-up soda can, which acts as a riser between the base and the socket. The hand-wired lamp socket was screwed onto the top of the threaded rod, and a bolt on the bottom sandwiched the entire base together, locking all the layers in place. A fabric disc was glued to the bottom to hide the drill and CNC access holes, and finally the lightbulb and shade were installed to complete the lamp.

Built during engineering IF at school in 2025.
